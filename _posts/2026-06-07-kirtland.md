        <p>
            The <a href="https://ebird.org/species/kirwar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Kirtland's Warbler (<i>Setophaga kirtlandii</i>)</a> is a small threatened songbird with a very peculiar range: it winters in the Bahamas and only breeds in Northern Michigan and Wisconsin. This limited distribution is caused by its limiting nesting requirements, as the species requires pure stands of young <a href="https://www.inaturalist.org/taxa/58724-Pinus-banksiana" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> Jack Pine (<i>Pinus banksiana</i>)</a>. Kirtland's Warblers are ground nesters and use the dense, low branches of Jack Pines to conceal their nest. As the trees get taller, the lower branches are lost and the patch becomes unusable to the warblers.
        </p>
        <p>
            Despite their low numbers, Kirtland's Warblers are pretty easy to find if you know where to go. Some members of my cohort and I traveled up to Roscommon, Michigan (the states first bird city, see right), which is home to the annual Kirtland's Warbler festival.
        </p>
